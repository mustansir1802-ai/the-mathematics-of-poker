Chapter I · Combinatorics
How rare is your hand?
A 52-card deck yields C(52,5) = 2,598,960 distinct 5-card hands. Each ranking's probability is the count of its valid combinations divided by this total.
C(n,k) = n! / (k! × (n−k)!)   // combinations without replacement
C(52,5) = 52! / (5! × 47!) = 2,598,960  total possible hands

Chapter II · Conditional Probability
The flop changes everything
Once 3 community cards are revealed, your probability of improving is conditional on 47 unseen cards. The Rule of 4 gives a fast mental approximation for two streets.
P(hit on turn) = outs / 47
P(hit by river) ≈ outs × 4%   // Rule of 4 approximation

Chapter III · Expected Value
The math behind every call
Pot odds define the minimum equity needed to call profitably. If your equity exceeds pot odds, the call is +EV. Every decision can be reduced to this arithmetic.
EV(call) = P(win) × (Pot + Call) − P(lose) × Call
Pot Odds = Call / (Pot + Call)   // break-even equity threshold

Chapter IV · Game Theory
Optimal bet sizing: Kelly Criterion
The Kelly Criterion identifies the exact fraction of your bankroll to bet for maximum long-run growth. Overbetting increases ruin risk; underbetting sacrifices edge.
f* = (b·p − q) / b   // Kelly fraction
b = net odds on bet, p = win probability, q = 1 − p

Chapter V · Law of Large Numbers
Why skill wins in the long run
Short-term luck swamps any skill edge. But as n → ∞, variance per hand (σ/√n) shrinks to zero while expected profit (edge × n) grows linearly — skill inevitably dominates.
σ / √n → 0 as n → ∞   // variance per hand shrinks
E[profit] = edge × n      // expected profit grows linearly
